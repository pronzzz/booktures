# The Clockwork Fox

In the village of Thimble Hollow, where the beech woods grew so close that the houses wore moss like green caps, there lived a clockmaker named Odile. She was a small, round woman with silver hair wound into a bun and a pair of magnifying lenses that clicked down over her eyes whenever she bent to her work. Her shop smelled of oil and pine shavings, and every shelf ticked.

One autumn morning Odile found a fox on her doorstep. It was made of brass and copper, no bigger than a cat, with amber glass eyes and a tail of overlapping bronze leaves. A tiny key stuck out of its side. When she turned the key three times, the fox sneezed, shook itself, and trotted straight under her workbench as if it had always lived there.

Her apprentice, a tall, gangly boy called Pim with ink on his fingers and a green scarf wrapped twice around his neck, would not stop asking questions. Who built it? Why did it come here? Odile only shrugged and handed him an oil can. "Things that tick find people who listen," she said.

That night the fox woke them both by scratching at the door. Outside, the beech woods were full of lights. Hundreds of little lanterns hung from the branches, swaying though there was no wind, and between the trunks moved shapes of brass and glass: owls with gear-wheel wings, hares with springs for legs, a stag whose antlers were made of clock hands.

They followed the fox deep into the woods to a clearing where an enormous clock stood among the roots, its face cracked and its pendulum still. The clockwork creatures gathered in a ring around it, silent and waiting. Odile pulled her lenses down, opened her leather tool roll on the moss, and began to work by lantern light while Pim held the screws in his cupped hands.

At dawn the great pendulum swung once, then again, and the whole forest began to tick. The lanterns went out one by one. When Odile and Pim walked home through the morning mist, the fox trotted ahead of them with its bronze tail high, and from that day on, every clock in Thimble Hollow kept perfect time.
