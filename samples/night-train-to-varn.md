# Night Train to Varn

The last train to Varn left the capital at eleven, and Detective Ines Calloway was the only passenger in the dining car. She was a lean woman in a grey trench coat, with a black bob cut sharp at the jaw and a thin scar running through her left eyebrow. Rain streaked the windows. Her coffee had gone cold an hour ago.

At midnight a man sat down across from her without asking. He was heavyset, in a rumpled brown suit and a bowler hat, and his left hand never left the battered leather briefcase on his knees. He introduced himself as Mr. Halloran, a salesman of fountain pens. Ines noticed that his shoes were far too expensive for a man who sold pens.

Somewhere past the river bridge the lights flickered and died. For eleven seconds the carriage was dark except for the blue flashes of the storm. When the lights came back, Halloran was slumped against the window, the briefcase was gone, and a single red fountain pen lay on the white tablecloth.

The conductor, a nervous young man with a waxed moustache and a cap too big for him, swore that no one had passed through the corridor. Ines walked the length of the train with her flashlight, past sleeping compartments and the clatter of the couplings, until she reached the luggage van at the very end, where the door stood open to the rushing night.

Inside, among the trunks and mail sacks, a woman in a long green evening gown sat on the briefcase, smoking calmly. "You're quicker than they said, detective," she told Ines, and held up a ticket stamped for a station that had been closed for twenty years.

The train slowed. Through the rain a platform appeared, lit by a single gas lamp, with a station sign too faded to read. The woman in green stood, picked up the briefcase, and stepped down into the fog. When Ines jumped after her, the platform was empty, and the only sound was the train pulling away behind her into the dark.
